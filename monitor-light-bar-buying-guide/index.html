How to choose the right monitor light bar

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Look for a monitor light bar with asymmetric lighting to avoid screen glare, a wide color temperature range (ideally 2700K to 6500K), and a clamp that fits your monitor’s bezel thickness. Brands like BenQ, Lume Cube, and Quntis are well-regarded for consistent performance, but always check your monitor’s top lip thickness (most fit models up to about 1.5 inches) before buying.

What does a monitor light bar actually do?

A monitor light bar sits atop your screen and casts a focused pool of light onto your desk without shining directly into your eyes or causing glare on the display. It’s designed to reduce eye strain in dim rooms, especially for late-night work or gaming sessions. Unlike a traditional desk lamp, a monitor light bar aims light downward using a specialized lens or baffle system. This “asymmetric” beam pattern is the key feature that makes them useful, without it, you’d just get a dull reflection off your screen.

What should I look for in brightness and color temperature?

Most monitor light bars offer adjustable brightness levels and a range of color temperatures from warm yellow (around 2700K) to cool daylight (around 6500K). Having that range lets you match the light to your room’s ambient lighting or your personal preference for reducing blue light at night. For pure readability, a mid-range brightness with a neutral 4000K-5000K setting works for most people. If you do photo editing or color-critical work, look for a model with consistent color rendering, though note that no monitor light bar is a substitute for a calibrated studio light. Top brands like BenQ and Lume Cube include auto-adjusting ambient sensors that adapt brightness to your room’s light level. This is a nice convenience but not a necessity; manual controls work fine if you’re willing to tweak settings occasionally.

Will a light bar fit my monitor?

Monitor light bars attach via a counterweight clip or a clamp that sits on top of the display. Most are designed for flat, standard-profile monitors with bezels up to about 1.5 inches thick. Some models include adapters for curved monitors or for monitors with a rear hump, like the iMac or Dell UltraSharp series. Before buying, measure the thickness of your monitor’s top bezel (including any rear protrusion). If you have a very slim bezel (like a modern laptop) or an ultrawide with a severe curve, look for a light bar specifically branded as compatible, many work fine but read owner feedback to be sure. Counterweight designs are more universal for thin screens; clamp-style bars tend to need a flatter top surface.

How easy is it to control and install?

Installation is nearly always tool-free: you place the bar on the monitor, adjust the clip, and plug in the USB power (the light bar draws power from a USB-A port on your monitor or computer, so no wall outlet needed). Setup takes about a minute. Controls vary: touch-sensitive buttons on the bar itself, a separate remote puck (like the BenQ ScreenBar uses), or a wireless remote. Touch controls are sleek but can be finicky if you need to adjust in the dark. A physical remote or knob is easier to use without looking. If you frequently change brightness or color temperature (e.g., switching from daytime work to evening browsing), a remote-style controller is worth the extra few dollars. For “set and forget” users, a simple touch bar is sufficient.

Are there any downsides I should know about?

Monitor light bars cast light down to about 12 to 18 inches in front of the screen, excellent for desktop work, but not enough to light up a whole room. If you need ambient room lighting, this won’t replace an overhead fixture. Some users report a faint “hot spot” or uneven light distribution near the edges of the bar. Higher-end models like the BenQ ScreenBar Halo handle this well, but budget bars (under a few hundred dollars) may have a less uniform beam. Also, if your monitor has a glossy screen, the light bar may create a slight reflection on the upper part of the display, this is rare with asymmetric designs, but it can happen. Check return policies if you have a glossy display.

What about wireless or premium features?

A few models offer wireless charging or a built-in USB hub on the bar itself, which is handy for keeping your desk tidy. Others include a backlight that casts a soft glow on the wall behind the monitor, reducing eye strain in dark rooms even further. Wireless connectivity (Bluetooth or Wi-Fi) is uncommon but exists on some premium bars, allowing you to control the light from your phone or smart home system. For most users, that’s overkill, USB power and manual controls are perfectly adequate. The real premium upgrade is build quality: aluminum construction, smooth dimming without flicker, and consistent color temperature across the whole beam. If you plan to use the bar daily for years, spending more on a well-reviewed brand pays off.

Frequently asked questions

Can a monitor light bar cause screen glare?

Properly designed monitor light bars use an asymmetric beam that shines downward, so they should not reflect off your screen. However, if your monitor has a very glossy finish, a small amount of glare may appear in the top corner. Checking reviews from owners with similar displays is the best way to confirm.

Do I need a curved monitor version?

Curved monitors with a severe bend (e.g., 1000R) may cause the light bar to sit unevenly or slip. Many standard bars include adjustable mounts or rubber grips to work with moderate curvature. For very deep curves, look for a model explicitly sold as “curved monitor compatible” or check that the clip width accommodates the curve’s thickness.

Are monitor light bars safe for my eyes?

Yes, when used properly. Quality bars have flicker-free drivers and adjustable color temperatures to reduce blue light at night. Position the bar so no direct light hits your eyes, most designs make this easy. They are generally safer than a desktop lamp that shines into your peripheral vision.

Can I use a monitor light bar on an iMac or laptop?

On an iMac, the thick rear housing behind the screen can be a challenge. Some light bars come with a special wedge or spacer for iMacs. For laptops, the light bar may be too heavy or the top bezel too thin. A few portable, lightweight models exist specifically for laptops, but most bars are meant for desktop monitors.

Do monitor light bars work with ultrawide monitors?

Yes, but you need to ensure the bar’s width covers the screen adequately. Most bars are 18 to 20 inches wide, which suits standard ultrawides. The real concern is the mounting: some ultra-wides have a curved back that may not accept a flat clip. Check the manufacturer’s compatibility list before buying.

Is a backlight worth it?

A backlight that shines on the wall behind the monitor can reduce eye strain by balancing the contrast between a bright screen and a dark room. It’s a nice bonus for late-night use but not a core feature. If you already have bias lighting on your monitor, a backlight on the bar may be redundant.

In shortPick a monitor light bar with genuine asymmetric lighting, a wide color temperature range, and a mount that fits your monitor’s thickness. Brands like BenQ and Lume Cube have a strong reputation for consistent quality, while budget options like Quntis offer decent performance if you’re willing to compromise on beam uniformity. Measure first, read owner feedback for your specific monitor model, and you’ll get a simple upgrade that makes late-night work far more comfortable.
